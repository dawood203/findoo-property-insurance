00 / Start with the problem

BRD v0.1 · 14 October 2026

The brief described insurance as difficult to understand, apply for and claim, with inadequate support and unclear intermediary costs. These were supplied problem statements; they were not validated market or interview findings.

The business direction

Make property insurance easier to understand, purchase, manage and claim. Put a guided, transparent claims journey at the centre, while keeping insurance outcomes conditional on approved policy terms and decisions.

The working framework

Build a seven-phase orchestrator with a dedicated agent and gate for each stage, reusable skills, BRD identifiers and a decision history. A phase passes review only through explicit user approval.

01

Research

✓ Gate 01 approved · 14 October

Read the BRD, unpack the business problem and identify provisional user needs, stakeholders, risks and dependencies. Give every explicit functional and non-functional requirement a stable reference, BRD-001 through BRD-031.

What we did

  • Separated documented facts, assumptions and open questions.
  • Prepared stakeholder discussion questions.
  • Recorded competitor research as an evidence gap.

What we produced

  • Research findings and business problem statement.
  • User needs, questions, assumptions and risks.
  • A 31-row requirements traceability matrix.
What the evidence allowed us to say

The repository contained a draft BRD, but no stakeholder interview records, user studies or competitor sources. Research findings describe the supplied direction and its gaps. Competitor and interview work was not claimed as completed.

02

Define

✓ Gate 02 approved · 15 October

Translate the research into a bounded definition: who the experience serves, what problems it addresses, and which requirements support each customer task.

What we did

  • Defined user problems and hypothetical proto-personas.
  • Mapped the three BRD journeys.
  • Preserved the BRD priorities rather than inventing scores.

What we produced

  • 24 functional and 7 non-functional UX requirements.
  • Scope record and candidate success measures.
  • Business-to-user traceability for all 31 requirements.
Personas, priorities and measurement

The personas remain hypothetical because direct user research was unavailable. Functional priorities preserve 19 Must, 4 Should and 1 Could requirement. NFR priority is unstated. Candidate measures have no approved baselines or numerical targets.

03

Information architecture

✓ Gate 03 approved · 15 October

Give the requirements a place in the experience. Organize the product around exploring cover, accessing a policy, starting and progressing a claim, and finding support.

What we did

  • Built the navigation and content hierarchy.
  • Mapped task entry, exit, exception and recovery states.
  • Kept emergency and human-support routes discoverable.

What we produced

  • 20 conceptual screen nodes, IA-01–IA-20.
  • Quote, claims and support task flows.
  • Requirement-to-screen destinations for all 31 requirements.
Architectural boundaries

The nodes are conceptual destinations. The architecture does not establish an insurer's real operating workflow, purchase transaction, identity provider or service-level commitments. These remain dependent on approved rules and source systems.

04

UI design

✓ Gate 04 approved · 15 October

Describe layout, hierarchy, components, interactions and recovery before committing to final visual decisions. The approved phase deliverables were detailed specifications; Material Design 3 and WCAG 2.2 AA were supplied later as the design direction.

What we did

  • Specified information hierarchy and component states.
  • Documented responsive and accessibility behavior.
  • Integrated the supplied M3 skill into the existing agent.

What we produced

  • Detailed UI, component and responsive specifications.
  • Accessibility review and requirement trace map.
  • A later standalone login sample for exploration.
Design system and artifact status

The M3 skill is referenced from project instructions. Approved Findoo brand token values remain unavailable. The login concept uses a sample theme; it is not a production authentication system or an approved full-screen suite. No Figma file creation is evidenced. Accessibility certification and responsive test completion are not claimed.

05

Usability testing

✓ Gate 05 approved · 15 October

Prepare test scenarios and review the available specifications for usability risks. Keep AI heuristic observations separate from participant findings and developer feedback.

What we did

  • Prepared seven scenarios spanning quote, policy, claims and support.
  • Reviewed navigation, content, states and requirement coverage.
  • Logged seven heuristic risks with severity and retest needs.

Evidence available

  • 7 AI heuristic findings: 5 High, 2 Medium.
  • 0 actual participant-session findings.
  • 0 developer-review findings; existing-screen comparison unavailable.
What Gate 05 approval means

The recorded approval covers the test plan and heuristic-review artifacts. It does not establish that usability testing occurred, that users succeeded, or that the proposed design is validated. All participant scenarios remain unexecuted in the available evidence.

06

Iteration

◷ Gate 06 · work in progress

Prioritize the issues, identify causes supported by the evidence, and turn each finding into a linked change proposal. Preserve unresolved work and define the evidence required to close it.

What we produced

  • CHG-001–CHG-007 linked to the seven issues.
  • Reviewable interaction annotations and issue dispositions.
  • Seven retest plans and a residual-risk register.

Next review inputs

  • Approved tokens, content and operational rules.
  • Rendered testable tasks and integration behaviors.
  • RT-01–RT-07 completion evidence.
Preparing to meet human expectations
The iteration proposals are ready for discussion. The next step is to gather the required retest evidence and confirm that the experience meets the agreed human expectations before moving to final UX validation.
Later inputs and what they resolve

The M3 skill and WCAG 2.2 AA direction resolve design-system selection and the target standard. The standalone login sample provides one exploratory screen. The next review will bring together the full rendered flows, approved operational content and retest results for all seven improvement opportunities.

07

UX validation & approval

○ Upcoming · human review milestone

The defined final stage will consolidate requirement coverage, test evidence, residual risks and external approvals into a decision-ready review pack. This upcoming milestone brings the project evidence together for human review and an explicit approval decision.

Planned activities

  • Validate final requirement and evidence coverage.
  • Review unresolved risks and approval conditions.
  • Record DPO and EO decisions as external approvals.

Planned deliverables

  • Validation report and final traceability status.
  • Approval pack and residual-risk register.
  • Final review presentation after authorized entry.
Approval and external evidence rules

Explicit user approval is required after a passing gate. DPO/EO approval must have external evidence. A Box upload cannot be marked complete without a receipt, verified link or confirmation. No external approval or upload evidence is currently recorded. This process-story page does not execute the final validation phase.
