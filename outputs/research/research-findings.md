## Evidence convention

## 1. Business problem analysis

**Documented problem statement:** Customers report that property insurance feels unaffordable, complicated to apply for, hard to claim, and poorly supported. They may also distrust an intermediary-heavy model with unclear commissions. The BRD links this to abandonment, inadequate cover, incomplete/late claims, repeated contacts, lower trust, churn, and higher manual acquisition/servicing costs. (BRD §2; P-01–P-05.)

The primary business goal is a simple, guided and transparent claim journey that enables a policyholder to start, track and progress a claim during a stressful event. Supporting objectives are understandable coverage, reduced avoidable application effort, comparable verified options, visible claim progress, and reduced unnecessary entry/contact while preserving accessible human help. (BRD §3.)

**Important boundary:** The BRD prohibits promises of premium, underwriting approval, coverage outcome, claim payment, payment amount, or time. Such outcomes remain conditional on approved terms, evidence, decisions and law. (BRD §1; BRD-012, BRD-019.)

## 2. User needs analysis

| Provisional user group | Documented need | Traceability |
|---|---|---|
| Residential policy shopper | Understand protection and material choices; complete, save and resume application; receive verified eligibility/option information. | BRD §4; BRD-001–BRD-010 |
| Active claimant | Receive emergency distinction/guidance, report an incident, manage evidence, know stage/owner/next action, receive updates and reach human support. | BRD §4; BRD-013–BRD-023 |
| Existing policyholder | Access policy information, dates, deductible, documents, permitted servicing actions and help. | BRD §4; BRD-011, BRD-021–BRD-023 |
| Support and claims operations user | Receive concise authorized case context and reliable handoff; internal tooling is not in MVP unless approved. | BRD §4, §6; BRD-022 |

The groups are provisional segments, not validated personas. No direct user research evidence is available.

## 3. Research analysis

### Documented opportunities

- The BRD specifies transparent claim progression: stage, next action, owner and update window when known. (BRD §5; BRD-017.)
- It requires clear coverage/cost disclosures and verified eligibility rather than generic sales claims. (BRD-001, BRD-007–BRD-012.)
- It requires self-service with human escape routes and a callback/case fallback when real-time support is unavailable. (BRD §5; BRD-021–BRD-023.)
- It requires accessible recovery, save/resume and clear evidence status across high-friction flows. (BRD-002, BRD-005, BRD-016, BRD-025–BRD-029.)

### Research gaps

- There is no validated customer research, baseline, target, task-frequency data, market-size data, or evidence that the stated problem applies to a defined market.
- The BRD has no confirmed operating model, geography, property segment, insurer/carrier, policy type, channel, language, support SLA, accessibility standard, or system of record.
- A sixth business problem is explicitly missing. (P-06; OQ-04.)

## 4. Competitor analysis

**Status: Not performed — no competitor names, products, URLs, screenshots, research, or evaluation criteria were provided.**

No claim about competitor capability, pricing, compliance, or market position can be made from the available evidence.

**Required research before comparison:** confirm target market and property segment; nominate competitor/comparator set; capture dated evidence from public product/claim/support journeys; define consistent comparison criteria (coverage explanation, quote disclosures, claim start/progress, evidence handling, escalation, accessibility and support); obtain legal/compliance review before making comparative marketing claims.

## 5. Constraints and risks discovered

The research identified the BRD dependencies and risks in `assumptions-and-risks.md`. Key constraints are verified/approved insurance content, carrier/partner integration, staffed support, privacy/security handling of claim media, and jurisdiction-specific rules. The BRD itself is a draft proposal and not legal, pricing, privacy, insurance, or claims approval. (BRD §§6, 9, 11, 13.)

## 6. Research conclusion

The BRD provides a coherent direction for transparent customer-facing residential claims and a simplified quote/application flow, but it is insufficient to validate a particular market, service model, competitor position, or compliance interpretation. Phase 1 is ready for human review only; it does not authorize Phase 2.
